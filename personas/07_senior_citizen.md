# Persona: The Senior Citizen

## Profile
**Name:** Dorothy "Dot" Williams  
**Age:** 78  
**Occupation:** Retired teacher, volunteers at library  
**Tech Comfort:** Low — has a tablet her grandson set up, uses email and Facebook  
**Time Available:** Flexible — but prefers in-person interaction

## Backstory
Dot has lived in Twinsburg since 1972. She taught 3rd grade at Bissell Elementary for 30 years. She's active at the senior center, knows everyone, and is the unofficial neighborhood information hub. She gets frustrated when "young people" assume everyone can figure out apps and websites. She wants to stay informed but technology keeps changing.

## Primary Use Cases

### 1. "What's happening at the senior center?"
- Programs, meals, transportation services
- Medical screenings, social events
- Simple list, not complicated calendar

### 2. "When is the next meeting?"
- City council, school board dates
- She might attend in person
- Needs address and time clearly displayed

### 3. "Help my neighbor"
- Neighbor asks: "When is bulk trash pickup?"
- She wants to look it up and share the answer
- Often helps others who are less tech-savvy

### 4. "Library events"
- Book clubs, author talks
- Friends of the Library meetings
- Used to get this in the newspaper, now struggles to find it

## Pain Points with Current System
- "The city website is too complicated"
- "Text is too small, buttons are too small"
- "I don't know where to click"
- "Why does everything need a password?"

## What Would Delight Dot
- Large, readable text and clear navigation
- Print-friendly views she can post on the fridge
- Simple answers to simple questions
- No login required for basic information

## Feedback on Current Design

### 👍 Likes
- All information in one place — no more hunting
- Public web app with no login required
- Can ask questions in plain English (if someone shows her how)

### 👎 Concerns
- No mention of accessibility (font sizes, contrast, screen readers)
- Is there a simple view or is it all fancy calendars?
- AI chatbot might be intimidating if not designed carefully
- Will it work on her older iPad?

### 💡 Suggestions
- Accessibility-first design (WCAG AA compliance)
- Large text mode / high contrast mode
- "Simple view" option: Just a list of upcoming events
- Print-friendly pages for those who prefer paper
- Clear, jargon-free language
- "Ask a question" should feel like talking to a librarian, not a robot
- Phone number for human help if technology fails

## Key Questions for This Persona
1. Is accessibility (WCAG) part of the design requirements?
2. Is there a simple/list view alternative to calendar view?
3. Can pages be easily printed?
4. Will the AI feel approachable or intimidating?
5. What's the fallback when technology doesn't work for someone?
